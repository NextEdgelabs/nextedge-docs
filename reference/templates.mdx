Copy these structures into the approved project workspace. Assign an owner and link the source work item. Use fictional examples in public documentation; keep customer, employee, and incident records in restricted systems.

## Project brief

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Problem and outcome | Who needs what to improve, and why? |
| Process owner | Who can confirm business rules and acceptance? |
| Delivery and technical owners | Who coordinates the plan and the design? |
| Delivery method | Waterfall, Agile with Kanban, Agile with sprints, or a documented hybrid; rationale and review date |
| QA, release, and support owners | Who verifies, deploys, and receives the handover? |
| Scope and exclusions | What is included in this increment? |
| Change approval | For out-of-scope work, link written approval from the designated Founder/CEO, SVP, or VP and the agreed financial and billing terms |
| Workflow and records | Actors, states, source systems, and important data |
| Success measures | Baseline, measurement method, and expected direction |
| Dependencies and risks | Integrations, access, data, open decisions |
| Acceptance and release | Evidence needed and the agreed release approach |


An agreed planning cadence does not change reporting lines, leadership approval rights, or calendar-time support SLAs.

```text
Subject: [Project or product] Change request - [short description]
Project/customer context and affected module:
Requester:
Current behavior:
Requested behavior and business reason:
Affected users and operational impact:

```

Support/delivery records the email thread, any assigned ticket reference, owner, and status. Link the work item, scope decision, approval, customer agreement, implementation, verification, release, and closure outcome to that record as applicable.

## Out-of-scope change request

Store the completed record in the restricted project workspace. Product Owner/Manager and Technical Operations coordinate the record; the designated Founder/CEO, SVP, or VP approves the work and its financial and billing terms.

```text
Change reference and project:
Originating support email thread and ticket reference, if assigned:
Requester and delivery owner:
Current agreed scope:
Requested change and business reason:
Deliverables, exclusions and acceptance criteria:
Engineering effort, dependencies and risks:
Impact on existing commitments and timeline:
Additional delivery cost and proposed customer charge:
Billing basis and currency:
Invoice trigger or milestone and payment terms:
Discount or no-charge exception, if any:
Finance review reference:
Leadership decision (Founder/CEO, SVP, or VP):
Approver, decision date and written approval reference:
Approved scope, amount and billing terms:
Customer agreement reference:
Work items and revised delivery plan:
Verification, release and closure references:
Billing reference and milestone evidence:
Reapproval reference if the change is revised:
```

Keep implementation on hold while approval is missing, rejected, deferred, or awaiting clarification. A customer request is not internal financial approval. Use the [scope-change workflow](/company/ways-of-working#handle-a-change-in-scope) to route the decision.

## Onboarding record

| Field | What to record privately |
| --- | --- |
| Role, manager, and buddy | Confirmed people and responsibilities |
| Initial project | Domain and first workflow to learn |
| Access | Requested, approved, verified, blocked |
| First day and first week | Activities, evidence, and open questions |
| 30/60/90-day outcomes | Agreed learning and contribution goals |
| Role-specific readiness | Relevant checks and functional owner |
| Follow-up | Support needed, next action, and review date |

## Incident review

Record the impact, timeline, detection, containment, recovery, data reconciliation, and contributing conditions. Assign prevention actions with owners and verification criteria. Focus on improving the system and process.
